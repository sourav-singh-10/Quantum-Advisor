    Why a committee

    One model agrees with itself. A committee has to convince a critic.

    Each agent has one job and its own tools. They hand work to each other through the Planner, so every claim can be traced to who made it and what data it used.

    StrategistDraft: HOLD

    Risk managerR1: cover the daily swings

    Disagreement on purpose

    The strategist looks for returns; the risk manager protects you. A draft goes back with numbered objections until it survives, for up to three rounds. Then the Planner rules.

    Every number has a source

    Prices, ratios and risk scores come from tools, not from the model's memory. A failed source is swapped, and text in headlines or PDFs that tries to steer the agents is removed and reported.

    The decision

    Worded for you. Checkable line by line.

    If you do not own the stock, a sell reads as avoid and a hold as wait. Underneath sits the evidence: a scorecard, the chart with targets and a stop-loss, where every risk point came from, and the debate that changed the answer.

    After the analysis

    It keeps watch while the market is open.

    Follow companies and upload your holdings once. Briefs and alerts arrive in the app, and on Telegram or email if you turn them on.

    1. 8:30

      Morning brief

      Last close, the 52-week range, overnight news and what to check before the open, for every company you follow.

    2. 9:00 to 3:30

      Alerts

      Checked every 30 minutes: a headline that can move the price, a move of 3% or more, or a level you set.

    3. 3:15

      Closing brief

      Today's profit or loss on your holdings, the biggest movers and the news behind them.

    4. 4 weeks later

      Track record

      Every call is checked against the price a month on, so you can see when the committee was right and when it was not.
