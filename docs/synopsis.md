# Quantum Advisor — Project Synopsis

**Problem.** A retail investor in India deciding whether to buy, hold or sell a stock has to combine several kinds of evidence: price trend and volatility, company fundamentals, recent news, the company's own filed reports, and — most often ignored — whether the stock suits *their own* age, experience, risk appetite, horizon and goal. These signals live in different places, are often out of date with one another, and frequently point in different directions. The investor is left to reconcile them alone.

**Solution.** Quantum Advisor is a multi-agent system that works like an investment committee. Each agent makes its own decisions with an LLM (Google Gemini) and uses tools for data and arithmetic, so numbers are fetched or calculated, never invented. The six agents in our approved proposal are all built:

1. **Market Analysis Agent** (`MarketAnalyst`) chooses its own price source, switches when one fails, and reads trend, RSI, volatility, trading volume and drawdowns.
2. **Fundamental Analysis Agent** (`FundamentalsAnalyst`) judges valuation, EPS, profitability and debt.
3. **Risk Assessment Agent** (`RiskManager`) scores risk against the investor's capacity and critiques the proposed decision.
4. **Financial News Intelligence Agent** (`NewsAnalyst`) rates each recent headline for sentiment and whether it could move the price.
5. **Financial Document Analysis Agent** (`DocumentAnalyst`) reads an annual report or results PDF the investor uploads: it searches for the statements, reads the pages it chooses, extracts the figures, flags audit problems, and challenges the Fundamentals agent when the document and the live data disagree.
6. **Portfolio Advisor / Decision Agent**, built as two agents so that conflict resolution is a real exchange: a **Planner** coordinates the work, re-plans when an analyst fails, rules on conflicts between analysts and breaks deadlocks, and a **Strategy** agent proposes BUY, HOLD or SELL and must answer each of the Risk agent's objections, for up to three rounds.

Supporting agents turn the answers into a profile (**Profile**), size the position from the investor's real budget (**Portfolio**) and explain the outcome in plain language (**Report**). A second workflow reviews an uploaded portfolio: each holding is priced live and a **Portfolio Review** agent calls each one add, hold, trim or review. When the Strategy or Risk agent is unsure, it can ask the Planner to send an analyst back for specific evidence (for example a one-year price view), and the answer goes back into the debate. The committee's call is shown for the investor's position: someone who does not own the stock sees Buy, Wait or Avoid. Text in news or uploaded PDFs that tries to give the agents instructions is removed and reported. Every message between agents streams live to a committee board in the web app and is saved as an execution trace anyone can open from a link. If the LLM is unavailable, each agent falls back to transparent rules and is marked as such. An evaluation script measures the system on 18 analyses and five provoked situations (`docs/evaluation.md`, `docs/scenarios.md`).

**Target user.** Beginner-to-intermediate retail investors on NSE who want a personalised second opinion that shows its reasoning, and students learning how investment decisions are made. It is an educational decision-support prototype, not a substitute for a SEBI-registered investment adviser.

**Scope change since the approved proposal.** All six proposed agents are implemented. We split the Decision Agent into a Planner and a Strategy agent, because the review of our proposal asked for "a full back-and-forth replanning cycle"; separating the two lets the trace show the Strategy and Risk agents arguing and the Planner arbitrating. We added the Profile, Portfolio, Report and Portfolio Review agents, a login with a saved investor profile, memory of past analyses, and a live trace viewer. After the core was built we added what an investor needs between analyses: a **Today** screen, a watchlist with company search, a **Market watch** agent that writes a morning brief at 8:30 and a closing brief on the user's holdings at 3:15, alerts during market hours (including price levels the user sets), delivery in the app, by email or on Telegram, saved goals and companies to avoid, and a **track record** that judges every past call four weeks later. These extend the same agents and tools. Our own evaluation then led to three changes to the analysis: agents can ask each other for evidence during the debate, the stock's outlook and the investor's fit are judged separately (a stock that is too risky for someone is "Wait", not "Sell"), and decisions are made repeatable (the same input now gives the same call in 9 of 9 repeats, up from 5 of 9). Accounts can sign in with Google, and data can be kept in a free Postgres database so it survives redeploys. Everything uses free services: Gemini's free tier, yfinance, Google News RSS, Render's free hosting and GitHub Actions.
